# Three-minute presentation script

Screen: `dashboard.html` open in a browser. Keep `executive_brief.pdf` ready as a backup. About 400 words at a relaxed pace.

**0:00 – The question (20 sec)**
"I analysed 15 customers to answer one question: where should we focus retention effort next quarter? Nearly half of them, 7 of 15, have churned."

**0:20 – The situation (25 sec)** *(point to the four tiles)*
"That is 46.7% of customers but 32% of monthly revenue, about $410 of $1,270. The customers who leave are newer, about 7 months versus 29, they raise four times as many support tickets, and they pay about half as much."

**0:45 – The five insights (75 sec)** *(scroll the insight cards)*
"One: contract type. All 7 month-to-month customers churned. None of the 8 on one- or two-year contracts did.
Two: timing. Everyone with 12 months or less of tenure left.
Three: support. Nobody with two or fewer tickets churned. Everyone with three or more did.
Four: plan. Basic lost 5 of 7, Enterprise lost none, so we lose many small accounts rather than big revenue.
Five: payment method. UPI and debit payers churn more, but I treat that as weak, probably a stand-in for contract and plan."

**2:00 – Be honest about certainty (25 sec)** *(point to the callout and limitations)*
"Important: month-to-month and three-plus tickets are the same seven people, so I cannot tell which one drives churn. And 15 customers is small; month-to-month churn could plausibly be as low as 59%. This is a strong lead, not proof."

**2:25 – The impact (20 sec)** *(drag the slider to 10, then 30)*
"If a retention programme saves 10 to 30 percent of would-be churners, churn drops from 46.7% to between 42% and 33%, keeping roughly $41 to $123 a month. That is an assumption to test, and it ignores programme cost."

**2:45 – The ask (15 sec)**
"I recommend a 90-day pilot with a holdout group: offer annual plans to month-to-month customers, add a first-year check-in, and reach out at the third support ticket. Please approve it, plus a data pull of cancellation dates and reasons."

## Recording tips
- Use any screen recorder (the free tiers of Loom or the built-in Windows/Mac recorder work).
- Aim for 2:45 to 3:15. Say the limitations out loud; that is the point of the exercise.
